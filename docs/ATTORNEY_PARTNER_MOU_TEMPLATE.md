## 1. Purpose
1.1 Matra operates a zero-knowledge platform. Survivors of sexual and intimate-partner violence use it to document their experiences and to choose, at their sole discretion, to share encrypted records with verified attorneys.
1.2 This MOU sets out how Partner participates as a verified attorney. Partner provides legal services to survivors who choose Partner, on a **pro bono** basis or as otherwise permitted in §7.
1.3 This MOU does not create a partnership, joint venture, employment, or agency relationship. It does not create an attorney-client relationship between Matra and anyone.

## 2. Roles
2.1 **Matra** will:
 (a) operate the platform in accordance with its published Security Whitepaper and Privacy Policy;
 (b) verify Partner's identity and licensure before listing Partner (§3);
 (c) list Partner in the verified-attorney directory for the jurisdictions and practice areas Partner designates;
 (d) not select, assign, or recommend a particular attorney to a survivor beyond presenting the directory. The survivor chooses. **[Counsel: confirm the directory design for RPC 7.3 purposes.]**
 (e) not direct, supervise, or interfere with Partner's independent professional judgment (RPC 5.4(c));
 (f) provide no legal advice to survivors.
2.2 **Partner** will:
 (a) independently decide whether to accept any matter, after its own intake and conflict check;
 (b) be solely responsible for all legal services, advice, and the attorney-client relationship;
 (c) comply with the Rules of Professional Conduct of each jurisdiction in which Partner practices;
 (d) use the platform only for matters shared by survivors who chose Partner.
2.3 **The client.** Any attorney-client relationship is solely between Partner and the survivor. Matra is not a party to it and receives no confidential information about the representation other than platform metadata (§6.3).

## 3. Verification and eligibility
3.1 Before listing, Partner will provide: full name; bar admission(s) and registration numbers; certificate(s) of good standing (dated within 90 days); organizational affiliation; practice areas; jurisdictions; a disciplinary history attestation; and proof of professional liability coverage [or confirmation of coverage through the organization's pro bono program].
3.2 Matra will check public bar records, confirm the identity of the account holder through a [video call / organizational attestation], and record the verification in an admin audit log.
3.3 Partner will use multi-factor authentication or passkeys on its Matra account when available, will not share credentials, and will verify the survivor's key fingerprint when requested.
3.4 Partner will notify Matra within **5 business days** of any suspension, disbarment, change in good standing, or loss of insurance. Matra may then unverify Partner immediately.
3.5 Matra will re-verify annually.

## 4. Response-time expectations
4.1 Partner will acknowledge each new share within **[3] business days**, either by contacting the survivor through the safe-contact method she specified or by declining.
4.2 If Partner cannot take the matter (capacity, conflict, practice area), Partner will promptly decline in the platform, so the survivor can choose someone else. Partner will not retain shared material except as §6.5 permits.
4.3 Partner will honor the survivor's **safe-contact instructions** (for example, "text only," "never leave voicemail") without exception.
4.4 These are good-faith targets, not guarantees to any survivor. Matra will present them to survivors as targets.

## 5. Conflicts of interest
5.1 Partner will run a conflict check before substantive engagement, using the minimum information needed (for example, the names of the person(s) responsible and the survivor's legal name if provided).
5.2 If a conflict exists, Partner will decline without reviewing the shared record beyond what the conflict check requires. Partner will treat any information received as prospective-client information under RPC 1.18.
5.3 **Escrow matches.** If Matra later operates a counselor-mediated matching program, any attorney who receives matched survivors must evaluate joint-representation conflicts (RPC 1.7) before communicating with them together.

## 6. Confidentiality and data handling
6.1 **Zero knowledge.** Partner acknowledges that Matra cannot read survivor content and cannot recover data if keys are lost.
6.2 **Scope and consent.** Partner will access only the record, evidence, and documents within the scope of the survivor's consent. Partner acknowledges that every access is logged and visible to the survivor.
6.3 **Metadata.** Matra may see that a share exists, its timing and status, and Partner's identity. Matra will treat this metadata as confidential and will not disclose it except as required by law (§6.7) or with the survivor's consent.
6.4 **Security.** Partner will store downloaded materials only in systems that meet Partner's ethical obligations to safeguard client information (RPC 1.6; reasonable-security guidance such as ABA Formal Op. 477R). This means encryption at rest, access controls, and no personal or unsecured devices.
6.5 **Revocation.** On revocation, or on declining a matter, Partner will stop using the platform copy. Downloaded copies are then handled as follows: (a) if no attorney-client relationship was formed, delete them within **[10] days** unless a legal hold applies, and confirm deletion in the platform; (b) if Partner represents the survivor, handle them according to the engagement and Partner's file-retention obligations. A survivor's revocation of platform access does not by itself terminate a representation.
6.6 **Minors and mandatory reporting.** The platform is for adults (18+). If Partner learns information that may trigger a reporting obligation (for example, N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.10), Partner will follow its own legal and ethical obligations and will not involve Matra. Matra cannot read content and has no role in such decisions. **[Counsel: add a protocol for historical-abuse disclosures.]**
6.7 **Legal process.** If either party receives a subpoena, warrant, or other request for data relating to a survivor, it will (where lawful) notify the survivor promptly so she can seek to quash, and will produce no more than legally required. Matra's response will be limited to what it holds (ciphertext and metadata) and will follow its published law-enforcement policy.
6.8 **Breach.** Each party will notify the other within **72 hours** of discovering a security incident affecting survivor data or credentials, and will cooperate on notifications required by law (N.J.S.A. 56:8-163; NY GBL § 899-aa).
6.9 **Grant conditions.** If either party is a VAWA- or VOCA-funded grantee or subgrantee, the parties will comply with 34 U.S.C. 12291(b)(2) and 28 C.F.R. 94.115. This includes releasing personally identifying information only with informed, written, reasonably time-limited consent.
6.10 **No marketing use.** Partner will not use survivor information or platform data for marketing, solicitation, or any purpose other than the survivor's matter.

## 7. Fees: no fee sharing, no referral fees
7.1 **Matra charges Partner nothing** for listing, referrals, or platform use, and will accept **no part of any legal fee**, settlement, award, or VCCO payment. Partner will pay nothing of value to Matra or its personnel for recommending Partner (RPC 5.4(a), 7.2(c), 7.3(d)).
7.2 Partner represents survivors **pro bono** unless the survivor agrees otherwise in a written engagement that complies with the RPCs. If a matter is eligible for a fee paid by a third party (for example, N.J. VCCO legal-assistance reimbursement under N.J.S.A. 52:4B-8(b) and N.J.A.C. 13:75-4.10, or statutory fee-shifting), Partner will (a) disclose this to the survivor in writing, (b) never charge the survivor more than the law allows (52:4B-8(a)(3)), and (c) report the matter to Matra as "not pro bono" in aggregate statistics.
7.3 Donations from Partner or its firm to Matra, if any, must be unrelated to and not contingent on any referral. **[Counsel: assess whether any donation policy is needed, or whether donations from participating attorneys should be barred entirely.]**
7.4 Court-awarded fee sharing with a nonprofit under NJ RPC 5.4(a)(5) **[VERIFY numbering]** is not contemplated. It may be addressed only in a separate written agreement reviewed by counsel.

## 8. Professional independence and communications
8.1 Matra will not review, edit, or direct Partner's advice or work product.
8.2 Partner will not describe Matra records to courts or opposing parties as "tamper-proof," "subpoena-proof," or "admissible." Partner may cite the ledger as described in the Security Whitepaper.
8.3 Partner may provide feedback on the intake script and brief format. Matra will consider it through its advisory review process.

## 9. Insurance and liability
9.1 Each party is responsible for its own acts and omissions. Partner maintains professional liability coverage as described in §3.1.
9.2 Matra provides the platform "as is" for the purposes described. It does not warrant that it is uninterrupted or free from defects. **[Counsel: indemnities and limitation of liability; consider nonprofit and pro-bono program norms.]**

## 10. Term and termination
10.1 Term: one year from the date above, renewing automatically for one-year terms.
10.2 Either party may terminate on **30 days'** written notice. Matra may suspend or terminate immediately for a breach of §§3, 6, or 7, a change in licensure, or a risk to survivor safety.
10.3 On termination: Matra will remove Partner from the directory and disable new shares. Existing representations continue under the engagement between Partner and the survivor. Partner will handle platform-derived data under §6.5. Matra will notify affected survivors that Partner is no longer listed, without disclosing the reason.
10.4 Sections 6, 7, and 9 survive termination.
