## 2. Conclusions

### 2.1 Claim: "the state reimburses victim's rights attorneys up to $275/hour" — **CONFIRMED, with important qualifiers**
- **Statute** (V2, N.J.S.A. 52:4B-8(b), as amended by P.L.2019 c.380): "The office may allow payment up to a maximum of $10,000, at an hourly rate of **$275 or more to be fixed by the office**, to an attorney who provides legal assistance to a victim in any legal matter arising out of the victimization, other than a decision of the [VCCO] involving victim compensation or any related appeal … **provided that the victim is otherwise eligible to receive compensation**." Payments count toward the overall compensation cap (52:4B-18 limitation).
- **Regulation** (V3, N.J.A.C. 13:75-4.10(c)): "Attorney fee awards made pursuant to (a) or (b) above shall be paid at an hourly rate of $275.00." Requires an attorney affidavit of services with a detailed hourly accounting. Rate history: $125 → $200 → $275 (current since 11/1/2021).
- **Application** (V1, Section 5): "Attorney fees (victims' rights in certain criminal and/or civil proceedings that are directly related to the VCCO claim) … payable at **$275 per hour not to exceed $10,000** maximum allowance." "Attorney representation with filing claim: … $275 per hour **or 15% of the total award, whichever is less**." (Statute 52:4B-8(a): ≤15% of the award, minimum $500 unless the attorney lacked diligence; $500 may be paid even if the claim is denied.)
- **Qualifiers that must be stated accurately in any pitch:**
  1. It is $275/hr **fixed**, not "up to"; the **cap is $10,000** per victim for victims'-rights / related legal matters, and it sits inside the overall $25,000 claim maximum.
  2. Paid **only if the victim is otherwise eligible for VCCO compensation** — which today means the crime was **reported to law enforcement within 9 months** (see 2.2). V5: "The VCCO will pay legal fees only if it awards compensation."
  3. Fees for helping file the VCCO claim itself are separately limited to the lesser of $275/hr or 15% of the award.
  4. It is reimbursement to the attorney on application with an hourly affidavit; it is discretionary ("may allow").

### 2.2 VCCO eligibility rules relevant to survivors (especially those who have NOT reported)
- **Reporting requirement (current law):** the offense must have been "reported to the police or other appropriate law enforcement agency within **nine months** after its occurrence or reasonable discovery" (N.J.S.A. 52:4B-18; V1, V4). Rule effective 8/1/2020 (previously 90 days).
- **No sexual-assault exception is in force (as far as we could confirm).** The only statutory carve-out we found is narrow: counseling via a hospital-based violence intervention program may proceed after a late report if the victim was admitted to the hospital (N.J.S.A. 52:4B-25.2(b)(3), V2). Pending bills **S1294 / S4985 / A6254** would let victims of *unreported* sexual offenses qualify if they received forensic medical services. **UNCONFIRMED whether S1294 has been enacted** as of 2026-10-06 — last status we could see: passed Senate committee 2/19/2026. The app must not tell survivors they qualify without a report; it should say "currently a police report within 9 months is required; a bill is pending".
- A Five-Year Hold Kit (forensic exam without reporting, P4) does **not** by itself satisfy the reporting requirement under current law. The forensic exam itself is free to the victim regardless (N.J.S.A. 52:4B-22(b): "victims will not be charged any fee for services … directly associated with a forensic sexual assault examination", V2).
- **Filing deadline:** within **5 years** of the injury; "good cause" can extend (including not being informed of VCCO benefits). Minors: 5 years runs from the 18th birthday.
- **Cooperation:** application says the victim must have "cooperated with police and prosecutor's office" (V1); eligibility page says "should cooperate with reasonable requests" (V4). Eligibility does **not** depend on arrest, prosecution or conviction.
- **Residency/jurisdiction:** crime occurred in NJ, or NJ resident victimized in a state without a program.
- **Payer of last resort:** insurance, charity care, restitution first.
- **Contributory conduct:** award may be reduced/denied for victim conduct contributing to injury (52:4B-18). NB 52:4B-60.2(c)(2) gives sexual-violence victims the right to be free of any suggestion of contributory negligence or assumption of risk.
- Other exclusions: offender/accomplice; outstanding VCCO assessments; outstanding indictable warrants or pending Superior Court charges.
- **Police report needed for VCCO counseling:** "You must have a police report or letter from the prosecutor's office … to be eligible for VCCO authorized mental health counseling" (V5).

### 2.3 VCCO benefit caps (V1 Section 5, V4)
Overall **$25,000** per claim (+ up to $35,000 supplemental for catastrophic injury). Counseling $20,000 (direct victim), $7,000 secondary victims; relocation $3,000; loss of earnings $600/wk, max 104 weeks; domestic help $6,500; child/dependent care $6,500; crime-scene clean-up $4,000; medical transportation $0.31/mi, ≤$10/day, ≤$3,000; court-attendance lost earnings $600/wk; attorney (victims' rights) $275/hr ≤ $10,000; attorney (claim filing) lesser of $275/hr or 15% of award; funeral $7,500 (n/a here). Not covered: pain and suffering, property loss (except clean-up).

### 2.5 Language rules adopted for the brief (P1, P2, P3)
1. The narrative and every survivor-authored field are reproduced **verbatim**, in a visibly distinct "In the survivor's own words" style, never summarized, reordered or corrected. Software-authored text is limited to labels and procedural notes.
2. Software labels avoid the language of consensual sex and of doubt ("alleged victim", "claims", "had sex"). We use "survivor", "person(s) responsible (as identified by the survivor)", "incident".
3. Statements made by the person responsible get their own section (IACP).
4. Sensory details and context before/after are captured (trauma-informed memory: sensory/peripheral details are often what is recalled most clearly).
5. Outcry / fresh-complaint witnesses (P6) get their own section with who/when/how/what was said.
6. Evidence is listed with integrity data (SHA-256) and an explicit note that metadata stripping removes device metadata from the shared copy — originals should be preserved.
7. The brief states it is **not a sworn statement unless signed**, and that the survivor controls participation (52:4B-60.2(c)(7)).

## 5. Gaps in `IntakeRecord` the orchestrator should consider adding

High value for the **police brief** (IACP / EVAWI / National Protocol items):
1. `incident.force` — physical force, restraint, threats, weapon(s) (`weaponInvolved?: boolean; weaponDescription?: string`).
2. `incident.strangulation?: boolean` — high-lethality indicator, explicitly asked in IACP/DV practice.
3. `incident.substances` — whether the survivor consumed / was given alcohol or drugs, and suspected incapacitation (complements `drugging_suspected`; affects toxicology time window). Must be phrased neutrally.
4. `incident.communicatedNonConsent` / how the survivor responded (free text, own words) — supports documenting trauma responses (freeze, compliance) without software interpretation.
5. `perpetrators[].{address, workplace, vehicle, currentWhereabouts, age/estimatedAge}`.
6. `medical.{toxicologyKit?: boolean; fiveYearHoldKit?: boolean; kitReportedToPolice?: boolean; examiner?: string}` (NJ Five-Year Hold Kits).
7. Physical (non-digital) evidence preserved: clothing, bedding, condoms, etc. (`physicalEvidence?: string`), and `evidence[].originalRetained?: boolean` (stripping metadata destroys EXIF time/GPS; originals matter).
8. `survivor.accommodations` (interpreter, disability) and `survivor.wantsAdvocatePresent`.
9. `incident.timezone` (or store times with offset) — ISO dates without zone are ambiguous.

Needed for **VCCO**:
10. `survivor.mailingAddress {street, city, zip}` and phone type (home/cell/work).
11. `survivor.sex`, `raceEthnicity`, `maritalStatus` (optional; VCCO demographic questions) — prefer "prefer not to say".
12. `claimant` — is the survivor filing for herself? If not: claimant name, relationship, contact (minors/guardians).
13. `alternateContact {name, relationship, phone, email}` (VCCO §3) — reuse for safe-contact.
14. `priorReports[].agencyType: 'police' | 'prosecutor' | 'campus_police' | 'title_ix' | 'other'`, `nameOnReport`, `prosecutorFileNumber` — required to evaluate the 9-month rule.
15. `perpetrators[].livedWithSurvivor?: boolean`, `restitutionOrdered?: 'yes'|'no'|'unknown'`.
16. `financialImpact` as structured entries `{category, amount: number, provider?, dates?, paidBy?}` rather than free strings; insurance `{type, carrier, policyNumber}`; `charityCareApplied?`.
17. `providers[] {name, type: 'medical'|'counseling'|'dental'|'pharmacy', address, phone, dates}`.
18. `employment {employer, address, phone, datesAbsent, onTheJob, appliedDisabilityOrFLI}`.
19. `attorney {name, address, phone, representationType}` and `intendsCivilSuit` (VCCO §10; Assignment of Interest implications).
20. `referralSource` and `goodCauseExplanation` (late report / late filing, VCCO §16).
