## Question

When several survivors match the same named person but only some consent to a case, may Matra or the counselor use the non-consenting survivors' reports, or tell the consenting survivors or their lawyer that others exist?

## Context

Founder (interview 2026-11-11): if three survivors match and only one wants to proceed, the case should go ahead, and asked "can we use the info from the others without actually involving them?" Each survivor's consent at lodging covers counselor contact only ([LEGAL_RESEARCH_MEMO.md §7A](../../LEGAL_RESEARCH_MEMO.md#7a-legal-options-counselor-model-privilege-and-solicitation)).

Build decision 2026-11-11 (added by research session; correct if wrong): the counselor (a licensed attorney) first
sees only the matched identifiers, each survivor's states and her chosen contact method; a survivor's full report is
decrypted for the counselor only after she agrees. So a non-consenting survivor's *report* is never in the counselor's
hands; what the counselor does hold is the fact that she exists, her identifiers for the named person, her states and
her contact method. Routing uses incident state and survivor state (Q-017, Q-035).

## Research

**1. Is the non-consenting survivor the counselor's prospective client?** If she is, the lawyer-confidentiality rules
below bind the counselor; if not, only Matra's own promises and privacy law do.
- *For:* NY Rule 1.18(a) and PA RPC 1.18(a): a prospective client is one who "consults with a lawyer about the
  possibility of forming a client-lawyer relationship with respect to a matter"; CA Rule 1.18(a) instead covers one who
  consults "for the purpose of retaining the lawyer or securing legal service or advice." Matra's opt-in names the counselor as a licensed
  attorney who will explain legal options, which supports a reasonable expectation of confidentiality. NJ ACPE
  Opinion 695 (2004) treats that expectation as "the determining factor" and gives prospective-client information
  "the same cloak of protection furnished to actual clients" ([op. 695](https://www.njcourts.gov/sites/default/files/notices/2004/03/OPINION695_02182004.pdf)).
- *Against:* she lodged an entry with a role, not a named lawyer, and may never be contacted or answer. NY Rule
  1.18(e)(1) excludes a person who "communicates information unilaterally to a lawyer, without any reasonable
  expectation that the lawyer is willing to discuss" representation. Whether a pre-match sealed entry is a
  "consultation" is untested (same gap as Q-011, LEGAL_RESEARCH_MEMO §7A) **[VERIFY]**.

**2. Revealing her existence to the consenting survivor or her lawyer.** Under NY Rule 1.18(b) a lawyer "shall not use
or reveal that information, except as Rule 1.9 would permit"; NY Rule 1.9(c)(2) permits revealing a former client's
information only as the Rules would permit for a current client
(22 NYCRR Part 1200; text read from the [NYSBA compilation as amended through July 1, 2026](https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NYSBA-NY-Rules-of-Professional-Conduct-as-amended-through-July-1-2026.pdf)). CA Rule 1.18(b) is to the same effect, tied to Bus. &
Prof. Code § 6068(e) and Rule 1.6 ([State Bar of California rule PDF](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/rules/Rule_1.18-Exec_Summary-Redline.pdf); effective Nov. 1, 2018).
NJ ACPE 695 (a firm asked whether to tell its corporate client that an employee had sought to sue it) reads the NJ duty to preclude disclosure of "the identity of the inquiring individual, *the fact of the
individual's contact*, and any information received in connection with the contact." On that reading even "two
others named him," with no names, appears to reveal protected information. Op. 695 says NJ RPC 1.18 (eff. Jan. 1, 2004)
prohibits "use or revelation" of prospective-client information; its current text was not reachable this session **[VERIFY]**. PA is narrower: PA RPC 1.18(b) bars use or revelation only of information "which
may be significantly harmful to that person" ([Disciplinary Board compilation](https://www.padisciplinaryboard.org/for-attorneys/rules/rule/3/the-rules-of-professional-conduct), amended through Nov. 14, 2024).
A bare count might fall outside PA's rule but not NJ's or NY's. The survivor's informed consent appears to cure this:
NY Rule 1.6(a)(1) and PA RPC 1.6(a) both except disclosure on the client's informed consent (texts read); CA and NJ
1.6(a) not checked **[VERIFY]**.

**3. "Using" her information without revealing it.** The Rule 1.18(b) cross-reference to Rule 1.9 matters: NY Rule
1.9(c)(1) bars using a former client's information only "to the disadvantage of the former client," unless generally
known. One reading: the counselor may *use* what she knows (for example, to judge that a consenting survivor's case is
worth pursuing) so long as nothing is revealed and the use does not disadvantage the non-consenter. The opposing
reading: a case against the named person that she declined to join may disadvantage her (retaliation, being drawn in
as a witness), so even internal use is risky. ABA Formal Op. 492 (June 9, 2020) confirms a Model Rule 1.18(b) confidentiality duty to
prospective clients but is mainly about "significantly harmful" information and conflicts, not this kind of use. Unsettled **[VERIFY]**.

**4. Using her report as evidence in another survivor's case.** Under the build it cannot be used without her: it is
never decrypted for the counselor. If it were, FRE 415(a) lets a civil court admit evidence that a party "committed
any other sexual assault," but 415(b) requires disclosure to the opposing party, "including witnesses' statements" at
least 15 days before trial ([FRE 415](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_415)). Using her report would expose it
and her to the named person. In practice she would also have to testify. NJ, NY, CA and PA analogues (e.g. N.J.R.E.
404(b)) not checked **[VERIFY]**.

**5. The counselor's divided loyalty.** If the counselor owes duties to both survivors, withholding a material fact
(others exist) from the consenting one while advising her may strain the duty to communicate (RPC 1.4) and create a
material-limitation conflict (RPC 1.7(a)(2)) **[VERIFY]**. Limited-scope options counseling (RPC 1.2(c)), disclosed
at the outset, is the mitigation the memo proposes (LEGAL_RESEARCH_MEMO §7A); whether it suffices is untested.

**6. Matra's own duties (non-lawyer).** If grant-funded under VAWA, 34 U.S.C. § 12291(b)(2)(B)(ii) bars grantees from releasing
"individual client information without the informed, written, reasonably time-limited consent" of the person
(local copy: `compiled_resources/08_.../VAWA_34USC12291_confidentiality_excerpt_LII.md`; see Q-009). Telling others
she exists, beyond what her consent covered, could also be a deceptive practice under 15 U.S.C. § 45(a)(1) or state
UDAP law, if Matra's privacy promise says otherwise; whether the FTC Act reaches a nonprofit is a separate question
**[VERIFY]**.

**Unsettled:** whether a sealed entry makes her a prospective client; whether a non-identifying count is "information"
of hers; whether consent obtained at lodging, before any match, is informed enough to cover disclosure of her
existence (Q-023, Q-032).

## Ask for counsel

We propose: (1) a non-consenting survivor's report is never decrypted or used; (2) at lodging, a separate optional
checkbox lets the counselor tell other matched survivors only "at least one other person named the same person,"
never who, how many or where; (3) without that box ticked, the counselor says nothing about her existence. Under NJ,
NY, CA and PA rules, is a matched survivor a prospective client of the counselor, and is (2) valid consent for that
disclosure, or must it be asked again at match time?
