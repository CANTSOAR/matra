## Question

When a survivor deletes her records (account deletion), what spoliation and N.J.S.A. 2C:28-6 (tampering with evidence) risk does she face depending on timing, and what warning language should Matra show?

## Context

'Delete everything' deletes her ciphertext and wrapped keys from the live database, after she types DELETE and re-enters her passphrase; Matra cannot restore them. It is not a crypto-shred: a server backup made before the deletion still holds them, openable with her passphrase. It shipped 2026-10-14 (apps/web, PRD M9; passphrase step and wording corrected 2026-11-13). Background: [LEGAL_RESEARCH_MEMO.md §2](../../LEGAL_RESEARCH_MEMO.md#2-discoverability-subpoenas-and-privilege).

Founder (2026-11-11): edits create a new version; she can withdraw or delete at any point, and should delete only information she thinks is wrong or harmful. Point for counsel: deleting a version after she anticipates litigation, or after release to a lawyer, is where spoliation risk is highest.
