# 06 — Statistics and Landscape: Sources

Collected 7 October 2026 for Matra (NJ-focused, zero-knowledge survivor intake; connects survivors with VCCO-paid attorneys).

**Purpose:** cite the core numbers correctly and know each one's caveats before a funder, reporter, legislator or defense attorney points them out.

**Page convention:** "p. N" is the page number in the PDF viewer. When the printed page number differs, it appears in parentheses, for example "p. 9 (printed ix)".

**Two kinds of data. Never mix them in one sentence without saying so:**
- **Survey data** (CDC NISVS, BJS NCVS, AAU) asks people directly, so it captures assaults that were never reported. NISVS reports **lifetime prevalence**, the share of people *ever* victimized. NCVS reports **annual victimizations** among people age 12 and older, using a crime-survey frame.
- **Police data** (FBI UCR/NIBRS, NJSP UCR) counts only crimes known to police. These numbers measure *reporting and police activity*, not incidence.

## Commonly misquoted stats

### "Every 68 seconds an American is sexually assaulted" (RAINN)
- **Basis:** This is not a measured interval. RAINN took an annual NCVS count of rape/sexual assault victimizations (a 5-year rolling average, ages 12+) and divided the seconds in a year by it. 68 seconds implies about 463,000 victimizations a year. See `rainn_scope_of_problem_2024capture.md`, which cites the NCVS 2019 release.
- **Current RAINN wording (2025–26):** "Nearly every minute, someone in the U.S. is sexually assaulted. Every nine minutes, that someone is a child." RAINN also gives "an estimated 423,020 people age 12+ experience sexual violence each year", based on NCVS 2020–2024 (`rainn_scope_of_problem_2026capture.md`).
  - Discrepancy to note: averaging the five annual totals in BJS CV2024 Table 1 (2020–2024) gives 443,634, not 423,020. RAINN may have used different vintages of the estimates.
- **Caveats:**
  - These are victimizations, not people.
  - The count excludes children under 12.
  - The NCVS crime frame undercounts compared with NISVS.
  - "Every N seconds" framing implies a precision the data does not have.
- **Safer phrasing:** "BJS estimates roughly 400,000–560,000 rape or sexual assault victimizations of people 12 and older each year (NCVS, 2020–2024)."

### "1 in 5 college women is sexually assaulted"
- **Origin:** The 2007 Campus Sexual Assault Study (Krebs et al.). It surveyed two universities and found about 19% of women reported completed or attempted sexual assault since entering college. This includes unwanted touching.
- **AAU 2019:** 25.9% of undergraduate women at 33 schools reported nonconsensual sexual contact by force or incapacitation. AAU warns against national generalization (`aau...pdf` p. 17, printed xvii; response rate 21.9%, p. 35).
- **BJS (NCVS) data is much lower:**
  - 6.1 per 1,000 female students per year (1995–2013), and 7.6 per 1,000 for nonstudents of the same age.
  - College women were *less* at risk than nonstudents (`bjs_rape_sa_college_age_females_1995-2013.pdf` p. 1).
  - BJS p. 2 explains why NCVS and the public-health surveys differ.
- **Safer phrasing:** "Campus climate surveys find that roughly 1 in 5 to 1 in 4 undergraduate women experience nonconsensual sexual contact during college. Definitions vary, and these surveys are not nationally representative."

### "2–10% of reports are false" (and the inverse "90–98% are true")
- **Origin:**
  - Lisak et al. (2010) reviewed 136 reports at one university over 10 years. 8 (5.9%) were coded as false. Combined with prior research, they concluded the prevalence is "between 2% and 10%".
  - EVAWI (Lonsway, Archambault & Lisak, 2009) reviewed the methodologically rigorous studies: MAD Project 7%; Clark & Lewis 6%; Grace et al. 8.3%; Kelly et al. 2.5% after applying the official criteria; Heenan & Murray 2.1%. They put the range at **2–8%**. Studies where police alone classified the cases ran higher; Harris & Grace found 10.9% (`evawi...pdf` pp. 2–3).
- **Common misuses:**
  1. Reading the remaining 90–98% as "proven true". In Lisak's sample a large share of cases did not proceed and could not be classified either way. *(The commonly cited breakdown is 44.9% "did not proceed", 35.3% proceeded and 13.9% insufficient information. These come from the paywalled article; verify against the full text before quoting.)*
  2. Equating "unfounded", "no charges" or "acquittal" with false. Those are separate dispositions.
  3. Citing Kanin (1994, "41% false"). EVAWI and Lisak (2007) show it relied on a single detective's judgment, with no independent review, under a police polygraph practice that pressured victims to recant (`evawi...pdf` pp. 1–2).
- **Safer phrasing:** "Methodologically rigorous studies estimate that 2–10% of sexual assault reports are false. That is the share shown false, not a measure of how many are proven true."

### "Out of every 1,000 sexual assaults, 310 are reported and 25 perpetrators are incarcerated" / "975 walk free" (RAINN)
- **What the current RAINN page says (archived March 2026; "Last updated: August 28, 2025"):**
  - "98% of perpetrators walk free. For every 1000 sexual assaults, 50 reports lead to arrests, 28 cases lead to a felony convictions, and only 25 perpetrators are sentenced to incarceration." This is cited to *FBI NIBRS 2012–2016 (2017)*.
  - "1 in 3 victims report… 310 out of every 1,000." This is now footnoted to *BJS Felony Defendants in Large Urban Counties, 2009 (2013)*; earlier versions cited NCVS 2015–2019.
  - See `rainn_criminal_justice_statistics_2026capture.md`.
- **RAINN changed these numbers.** The June 2024 and mid-2025 version of the same page had a different funnel: 310 reported → 57 arrests → 13 referred to prosecutors → 7 felony convictions → 6 incarcerated ("995 will walk free"). See `rainn_criminal_justice_statistics_2024capture.md`.
- **Caveats:**
  - The funnel is a composite stitched together from different datasets, years and populations: NCVS reporting, NIBRS arrests, and felony case processing in large urban counties.
  - RAINN itself described it as combining several federal reports into an estimate.
  - It does not follow a cohort of cases.
  - 975 out of 1,000 is 97.5%, not "98%".
- **Safer alternative:** cite the cohort study.
  - Morabito, Williams & Pattavina (2019), six jurisdictions: 18.8% of reports led to arrest, about 6.5% to conviction, 1.5% to trial.
  - Phrase it as: "Most reported sexual assaults never result in arrest, and only a small fraction end in conviction." If you use RAINN's figure, say "RAINN estimates…" and note that it is a composite.

### "1 in 6 women" (RAINN) vs. "1 in 5" / "1 in 4" (CDC)
- RAINN's "1 in 6" comes from the **1998 NVAWS** (Tjaden & Thoennes), which is nearly 30 years old.
- Use CDC NISVS instead:
  - 2023/24: 21.0% (about 1 in 5)
  - 2016/17: 26.8% (about 1 in 4)
- The difference between NISVS editions is methodological, not a real trend. Name the year.

### "Only X% of rapes are reported" using the 2024 NCVS figure (23.6%)
- The reporting share was 46.0% in 2023 and 23.6% in 2024. That swing is mostly sampling noise on a small subsample (`bjs_criminal_victimization_2024.pdf` p. 7).
- Use the range, or a multi-year average such as 36% for 2005–10.

### "Clearance rate = solved"
- FBI and NJSP "cleared" figures include exceptional clearances, where no one is arrested.
- A 33% (national) or 19–21% (NJ) clearance rate overstates how many cases end in an arrest (`fbi_cius_2019_clearances.md`; Morabito p. 10).

### "Native women are 2.5x more likely to be raped"
- This older figure comes from 1990s–2000s BJS NCVS reports. They are not in this folder, so verify them before use.
- The newer NIJ estimate, based on NISVS 2010:
  - AI/AN women are **1.2 times** as likely as non-Hispanic White women to have experienced violence of any type in their lifetime.
  - They are **1.7 times** as likely in the past year (`nij_rosay_2016...pdf` p. 14).
- Do not use the "2.5x" figure without the original citation.

### 1. CDC NISVS 2023/2024 Sexual Violence Data Brief

- **Caveats:**
  - CDC explicitly cautions against comparing these figures with earlier NISVS years (p. 6, printed 4, "Limitations").
  - The survey excludes people who are institutionalized or homeless.
  - State estimates have wide confidence intervals.
  - It covers adults 18+ only.

### 5. BJS Female Victims of Sexual Violence, 1994–2010 (reporting, reasons, relationship)
- **File:** `bjs_female_victims_sexual_violence_1994-2010.pdf` (17 pp.)
- **Citation:** Planty M, Langton L, Krebs C, Berzofsky M, Smiley-McDonald H. *Female Victims of Sexual Violence, 1994–2010.* BJS Special Report NCJ 240655, March 2013 (revised 5/31/2016).
- **URL:** https://bjs.ojp.gov/content/pub/pdf/fvsv9410.pdf

- **Key numbers:**
  - **Sample:** 181,752 respondents out of 830,936 sampled, across 33 schools. (p. 7, printed vii) Response rate 21.9%. (p. 35)
  - **Nonconsensual sexual contact by force or incapacitation since enrollment:** 13.0% overall; undergraduate women 25.9%; graduate/professional women 9.7%; undergraduate men 6.8%. (p. 9, printed ix)
  - **Undergraduate women by school:** 14% to 32%. (p. 11)
  - **Trend at the 21 schools in both surveys (2015 to 2019):** +3.0 points to 26.4% for undergraduate women. (p. 11)
  - **Penetration (undergraduate women):** 7.3% by physical force, 5.4% by incapacitation. (p. 13)
  - **Help-seeking:** only 29.5% of women who experienced penetration contacted a program or resource. Top reasons for not contacting one: "could handle it myself" 20.0%; "not serious enough" 16.8%; embarrassed or ashamed 15.9%. (p. 13)
  - **Harassment:** 41.8% experienced at least one sexually harassing behavior. (p. 13)
- **Caveats:**
  - The schools were not random. AAU states the results should not be generalized beyond these schools.
  - Response bias is possible, though AAU's analysis found little evidence that it explains the high rates (p. 17).
- **2024:** No 2024 AAU aggregate report was found. AAU's campus climate page lists only the 2015 and 2019 surveys. Some institutions ran a 2024 Westat-administered survey (e.g., Pitt's "Higher Education Sexual Misconduct & Awareness Survey"), but no national aggregate was located.

### 12. Morabito, Williams & Pattavina (2019): NIJ sexual assault case attrition ("justice funnel")
- **File:** `nij_morabito_2019_sa_case_attrition.pdf` (237 pp.)
- **Citation:** Morabito MS, Williams LM, Pattavina A. *Decision Making in Sexual Assault Cases: Replication Research on Sexual Violence Case Attrition in the U.S.* Final report to NIJ, grant 2012-IJ-CX-0052, NCJ 252689, February 2019.
- **URL:** https://www.ncjrs.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/252689.pdf (via Internet Archive). Abstract: https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/decision-making-sexual-assault-cases-replication-research-sexual
- **Key numbers** (abstract p. 3; executive summary p. 10):
  - **Sample:** 3,269 complaints across 6 jurisdictions (2008–10). The analysis covers 2,887 female victims.
  - **Police outcomes:**
    - 48.6% cleared: 18.8% (544) by arrest and 29.8% (860) by exceptional means
    - 42.1% open/inactive
    - 7.3% (212) unfounded
  - **Prosecution:** charges were filed against 72% of arrested adults and declined for 22.8%.
  - **Convictions:**
    - 189 cases ended in a guilty verdict; 81% of those were plea bargains.
    - 7 bench convictions and 25 jury convictions.
    - 11 jury acquittals.
    - 43 cases (1.5%) were tried.
  - **Derived figures (my arithmetic):** 189/2,887 = 6.5% of reports ended in conviction. Trial convictions were 32/2,887 = 1.1%.

  - A cooperative victim was the strongest predictor of arrest.
- **Caveats:**
  - Not a national sample.
  - Female victims only.
