# Sources: Trauma Neurobiology and Cognitive Interviewing

Compiled 2026-10-06 for README section 1 ("Trauma Neurobiology & Cognitive Interviewing").
Every downloaded file was checked with `file` and opened to confirm it is the real PDF. None of these copies are pirated. NIJ material is a U.S. government work. EVAWI material is free to download but is EVAWI's copyrighted work, not public domain. The other papers are author-posted or repository copies. The PDFs themselves are not committed to git (see `../README.md`).

## 1. EVAWI: Understanding the Neurobiology of Trauma and Implications for Interviewing Victims

- **File:** `EVAWI_Neurobiology_of_Trauma_Interviewing_2023.pdf` (43 pp.)
- **Citation:** Wilson, C., Lonsway, K. A., & Archambault, J. (2016, updated August 2023). *Understanding the Neurobiology of Trauma and Implications for Interviewing Victims.* End Violence Against Women International (EVAWI) training bulletin. Free from EVAWI (copyrighted, not public domain).
- **URL:** https://evawintl.org/resource_library/evawi-training-bulletin-understanding-the-neurobiology-of-trauma-and-implications-for-interviewing-victims/
- **Notes:** Explains how the stress response (prefrontal impairment, habit and reflex behavior, freeze and tonic immobility, fragmented encoding) shapes what victims remember and how they act. It also lays out the practical consequences: memory is often non-linear and full of sensory and central detail, and gaps or inconsistencies are not proof of deception. For a question tree, this is the justification for leading with open free recall, allowing non-chronological entry points, offering sensory and "what were you thinking or feeling" prompts, and never penalizing "I don't know".
- **Discrepancy:** The README gives the title as "...Implications for **Victim Interviewing**". The actual title is "...Implications for **Interviewing Victims**". The current edition is August 2023.

## 2. EVAWI: Effective Victim Interviewing (EVAWI's interviewing module)

- **File:** `EVAWI_Effective_Victim_Interviewing_2023.pdf` (140 pp.)
- **Citation:** Lonsway, K. A., & Archambault, J. (with Frances, G., & Huhtanen, H.) (August 2021, updated December 2023). *Effective Victim Interviewing: Helping Victims Retrieve and Disclose Memories of Sexual Assault.* EVAWI OnLine Training Institute module. Free to download (copyrighted, not public domain).
- **URL:** The copy saved locally is the PDF mirrored by Lansing Community College: https://www.lcc.edu/consumer-information/documents/effective-victim-interviewing-evawi-modul.pdf. EVAWI's own course outline is at https://evawintl.org/wp-content/uploads/Course-Outline-Effective-Victim-Interviewing.pdf. EVAWI's OnLine Training Institute (free registration) is at https://evawintl.org.
- **Notes:** This is EVAWI's main guide to interviewing technique. It covers interview stages, rapport, "tell me everything you remember" free recall, open-ended follow-ups, sensory and peripheral-detail prompts, avoiding "why" questions, and how to document force, threat and incapacitation. It cites Fisher & Geiselman (2010) and FETI (Forensic Experiential Trauma Interview). For the intake app, it is the most direct source for question wording and ordering (open, then focused, then clarifying), and for the elements of a report that matter later in court.
- **Discrepancy:** EVAWI's own interviewing material is titled "Effective Victim Interviewing" (it was earlier an "Interviewing the Victim" OLTI module). It never uses the term "CI-AT". See item 3.

## 3. Cognitive Interview Adapted to Trauma (CI-AT): this name is real, but the protocol is not EVAWI's

- **File:** `Fisher_Hopper_CI-AT_Adapting_Cognitive_Interview_Sexual_Assault.pdf` (38 pp., author-posted manuscript)
- **Citation:** Fisher, R. P., Hopper, J. W., Bibeau, L., Chez, J., & Viau-Quesnel, C. (in press / 2026). Adapting the Cognitive Interview for victims of sexual assault. Chapter submitted for *Forensic Interviews Regarding Adult Sexual Assault Complainants: A Guide to Evidence-Based Practice* (W. O'Donohue & R. Bull, Eds.).
- **URL:** https://jimhopper.com/pdf/fisher_et_al_2026.pdf. A related practitioner article is "Trauma-Informed Interview Adaptations", *Police Chief* magazine: https://www.policechiefmagazine.org/trauma-informed-interview-adaptations/
- **Notes:** This chapter is the primary description of the CI-AT. It combines Fisher's Cognitive Interview with Hopper's account of how stress affects memory and with Sûreté du Québec investigators' field practice. The protocol has six phases: introduction, entirely free recall, recall through the five senses and interoceptive sensations one at a time, recall by segments, final verification, and conclusion. It also reports survey data from trained officers. It maps almost directly onto an intake question tree: an open narrative first, then optional sensory and body-sensation prompts, then segment-by-segment clarification, then a check-back with the victim.
- **Discrepancy:** The README calls CI-AT an EVAWI guide. CI-AT was developed by the Sûreté du Québec (Quebec Provincial Police; Bibeau, Chez) together with Fisher and Hopper. EVAWI presents it at conferences (for example, Bibeau and Hopper in April 2024) and runs CI-AT trainings, but it did not write the protocol and has no "CI-AT guides" of its own. Cite Fisher, Hopper et al. as the source.

## 4. Fisher & Geiselman (1992): the original Cognitive Interview book

- **File:** not downloaded (copyrighted book, no legal free copy)
- **Citation:** Fisher, R. P., & Geiselman, R. E. (1992). *Memory-Enhancing Techniques for Investigative Interviewing: The Cognitive Interview.* Springfield, IL: Charles C. Thomas. ISBN 978-0398057398.
- **URL:** NCJRS abstract: https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/memory-enhancing-techniques-investigative-interviewing-cognitive. Buy from Charles C. Thomas (ccthomas.com) or used booksellers, or borrow through WorldCat / interlibrary loan.
- **Notes:** This is the canonical manual. It sets out context reinstatement, "report everything", varied retrieval orders and perspectives, witness-compatible questioning, and not interrupting. Items 3, 5 and 6 cover its techniques well enough for product design, so you don't need to buy it unless you want the original step-by-step scripts.

## 5. Fisher & Geiselman (2010): Cognitive Interview and Therapeutic Jurisprudence

- **File:** not downloaded. A free author-posted copy exists on SSRN, but SSRN's bot protection blocked automated download. Open the URL below in a browser to get it.
- **Citation:** Fisher, R. P., & Geiselman, R. E. (2010). The Cognitive Interview method of conducting police interviews: Eliciting extensive information and promoting Therapeutic Jurisprudence. *International Journal of Law and Psychiatry, 33*(5-6), 321-328. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijlp.2010.09.004
- **URL:** Free on SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1696130. Publisher (paywalled): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijlp.2010.09.004
- **Notes:** A short, readable summary of the CI's components and evidence base. It argues that the same techniques that get more accurate information (witness control of the narrative, open questions, no interruptions, respect) also improve the witness's well-being. That makes it the best single citation for the claim that a non-leading, witness-led intake is both better evidence and less re-traumatizing.
- **Discrepancy:** The README cites "their **2014** paper on Therapeutic Jurisprudence". I found no 2014 Fisher & Geiselman paper on therapeutic jurisprudence. The paper that matches is this **2010** article in IJLP. The README also says this paper teaches "leading questions vs. non-suggestive sensory prompts". The 2010 paper covers open versus closed and leading questions within the CI, but the sensory-prompt material comes mainly from the CI-AT and EVAWI sources (items 2 and 3).

## 6. Memon, Meissner & Fraser (2010): Cognitive Interview meta-analysis

- **File:** `Memon_2010_Cognitive_Interview_meta-analysis.pdf` (accepted manuscript, Royal Holloway repository)
- **Citation:** Memon, A., Meissner, C. A., & Fraser, J. (2010). The Cognitive Interview: A meta-analytic review and study space analysis of the past 25 years. *Psychology, Public Policy, and Law, 16*(4), 340-372. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020518
- **URL:** https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/2f9938b0-c01b-7310-9ec8-e0aee92c9d17/9/
- **Notes:** A study-space analysis of 65 experiments and a meta-analysis of 46 papers. The CI produces a large, significant increase in correct details with only a small increase in errors and no difference in confabulation. Modified CI versions perform just as well. This is the evidence base for a CI-style intake. The small rise in errors is the trade-off to design around, for example with a final verification and check-back step.

## 7. NIJ / Rebecca Campbell (2012): The Neurobiology of Sexual Assault

- **File:** `NIJ_Campbell_2012_Neurobiology_Sexual_Assault_transcript.pdf` (14 pp., official NIJ transcript, from the PREA Resource Center mirror. The nij.ojp.gov PDF URL now redirects to an HTML page.)
- **Citation:** Campbell, R. (2012, December 3). *The Neurobiology of Sexual Assault: Implications for First Responders in Law Enforcement, Prosecution, and Victim Advocacy.* NIJ Research for the Real World seminar. National Institute of Justice. NCJ 240953.
- **URL:** NIJ page: https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/neurobiology-sexual-assault-implications-first-responders-law-enforcement. HTML transcript: https://nij.ojp.gov/transcript-neurobiology-sexual-assault. Video (1:34:57): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=khUfN58RUo8
- **Notes:** This is the widely cited lay explanation of how the stress-hormone flood and the fear circuitry produce flat affect, tonic immobility, and fragmented, non-sequential memory. In the Q&A, Campbell says the science would lead her to "put a substantial bet" on waiting about two sleep cycles before an in-depth interview, and she stresses supportive, non-judgmental questioning. For the app, it supports letting users save partial accounts and come back to them later, and avoiding any wording that implies a "correct" reaction (for example, "why didn't you...").
- **Gaps:** NIJ does not publish separate slides. They appear only in the video, and Campbell's longer slide deck was not published. NIJ has no stand-alone "research brief" by Campbell on this topic; the seminar page is the official product.

## 8. Tonic immobility: Möller, Söndergaard & Helström (2017)

- **File:** not downloaded. The article is free to read on Wiley (Unpaywall lists the publisher's free version), but Wiley's bot protection returns 403 to automated downloads. Open the link in a browser.
- **Citation:** Möller, A., Söndergaard, H. P., & Helström, L. (2017). Tonic immobility during sexual assault: A common reaction predicting post-traumatic stress disorder and severe depression. *Acta Obstetricia et Gynecologica Scandinavica, 96*(8), 932-938. https://doi.org/10.1111/aogs.13174
- **URL:** https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/aogs.13174 (PDF: https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/aogs.13174)
- **Notes:** In 298 women seen at a Stockholm rape emergency clinic, 70% reported significant tonic immobility and 48% reported extreme tonic immobility. Tonic immobility predicted PTSD (OR about 2.75) and severe depression (OR about 3.4) at 6 months. This is the strongest clinical evidence that "not resisting" is normal. The intake tree should ask neutral questions about what the body did (for example, "Were you able to move or speak?") and should not ask about resistance in a way that implies fault.

## README discrepancies (summary)

1. EVAWI bulletin title: the actual title is "...Implications for **Interviewing Victims**", not "...for Victim Interviewing". The current version is August 2023.
2. CI-AT: the name is real, but it is a Sûreté du Québec / Fisher / Hopper protocol, not EVAWI's own guide. EVAWI's interviewing material is "Effective Victim Interviewing".
3. Fisher & Geiselman therapeutic jurisprudence paper: it is from **2010** (IJLP 33:321-328), not 2014. I found no 2014 paper.
4. Campbell / NIJ: there is a seminar video and transcript, but no separate published slides or research brief.
